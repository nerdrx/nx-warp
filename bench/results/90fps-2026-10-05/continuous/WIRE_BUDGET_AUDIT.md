# ASTC rate target versus wire budget: source audit

**Source-only result: the FEC payload reserve is accounted for; the delivery-rate concurrency assumption remains unverified.** The ASTC target is a per-encoded-stream compressed-media budget, not a complete PHY/wire ceiling. The controller sums frame payload bytes (including parity) across streams, while `close_frame` divides by the maximum individual stream receive span (`bitrate_controller.cpp:416-437`). That can be a lower-bound capacity sample only under the relevant stream timing/concurrency assumptions; this audit did not verify those assumptions for serial native UDP sender jobs. A separate stereo-rate-scope CPU gate owns that question; do not duplicate it here. Fixed protocol/IP headers and requested repair payloads are outside the ordinary frame-byte counter. (NX direct mode has a separate observed quality-to-payload conversion; that is not the native ASTC path.)

Subsequent actual-controller replay and source correction are recorded in [Stereo delivery-rate accounting](stereo-rate-scope/README.md). This audit retains the historical dc012b1 scope; it does not describe the corrected source as still using the maximum-span rate denominator.

## Interpretation and scale

The configured/controller bitrate is a media/datagram budget contract, not an exact Ethernet/Wi-Fi PHY ceiling. FEC parity is deliberately accounted at the payload level. Native ASTC has no separate compression-ratio conversion in BBR: its adaptive ASTC rung compares actual compressed packet bytes to the per-stream budget, while BBR adjusts that budget from observed payload delivery and applies the FEC share. Remaining per-datagram IP/UDP and outer framing is absent from the byte numerator. The FEC comments’ under-0.2% estimate concerns the *incremental* UDP/IP headers from sending additional data shards after reserving FEC payload, not all packet headers (`common/fec.h:104-107`). Their airtime impact is not represented as bytes in the numerator. Whether aggregate payload bytes divided by maximum individual stream span is a valid lower-bound sample for serial native UDP jobs is a separate unverified concurrency question, owned by the active stereo-rate-scope CPU gate.

Illustrative arithmetic only, not a measured stream: a 100 Mbit/s aggregate payload budget at 90 fps corresponds to 138,889 bytes/frame before stream splitting. With K=8, the encoder-data share is 8/9 (about 88.9 Mbit/s) and the parity allowance is 1/9 (about 11.1 Mbit/s). IP/UDP and WiVRn framing are outside that payload arithmetic. This does not describe any captured native frame or headset result.

This audit did not measure whether header omission or retransmission traffic materially reduces high-rate headroom. Retransmissions are separately capped and their NACK/loss counts feed FEC adaptation. Do not infer stream-rate validity, pacing behavior, or queue impact from the synthetic FEC CPU probes or the older fixed-ratio recovery simulation.

**Decision:** preserve the existing FEC data-share reserve; do not add an arbitrary overhead multiplier. This source-only audit does not validate the frame-byte / max-stream-span concurrency assumption or establish a material rate defect. The active stereo-rate-scope CPU gate owns that check. A strict PHY ceiling would require separately measured per-path wire-byte accounting and a revised controller contract.
