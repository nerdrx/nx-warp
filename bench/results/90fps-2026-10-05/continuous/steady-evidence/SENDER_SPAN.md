# Sender-span metadata audit

Source `412a2bfe9ef54333fdc35cf9919c6443fa409909`; read-only. Per-eye feedback already carries `frame_index`, `stream_index`, `send_begin/end`, and `received_first/last_packet` (`common/wivrn_packets.h:940–955`). The shard accumulator copies timing from the terminal shard into feedback (`client/decoder/shard_accumulator.cpp:457–463`).

For normal shard sends, `video_encoder::SendData` stamps `send_begin` at the first shard and `send_end` at the final payload after pacing, immediately before sending it (`server/encoder/video_encoder.cpp:903–928, 960–970`). NX Warp carries the same timing fields only on its final datagram; its `send_end` is sampled immediately before `SendPacket` (`server/encoder/video_encoder_nxwarp.cpp:3017–3023, 3073–3089`). Server stamps are converted to the headset clock; the base encoder documents same-clock subtraction and uses XrTime deltas as nanoseconds (`server/encoder/video_encoder.h:165–170, 202–204`; `server/driver/clock_offset.cpp:157–160`).

This is sufficient metadata to compute a matched per-eye candidate `max(send_end-send_begin, received_last-received_first)` without new fields, provided all four stamps are nonzero and each span positive. Current BBR instead stores per-stream receive bounds and unions receive intervals (`server/driver/bitrate_controller.cpp:839–853, 440–478`); this is feasibility, not evidence the alternative improves estimates. Incomplete frames lack `sent_to_decoder` and are treated as lost, not rate samples (`bitrate_controller.cpp:831–855`). Repair-completed frames may contribute if timing survives on the terminal shard; zero/partial spans must be excluded. No policy win is established.
