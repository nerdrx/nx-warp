# Probe bounding audit

Read-only audit at source revision `6902940fcd1cb15cef2337e9d6ab85cc266ebbd4`. I read `stereo-rate-coupled/README.md` and its production `pacing_slot` trace first. That replay assumes fully ready eye pairs and serialized payload service; its 10.16 s baseline overrun is a model result, not observed Wi-Fi backlog or a missed-frame measurement.

## Existing bounds and mode differences

- BBR is not forced on: server config defaults to AIMD, and the headset's optional `bitrate_bbr` selector wins over that default (`server/driver/configuration.cpp:237-247`, `client/configuration.h:77-83`, `client/configuration.cpp:504-505`, `server/driver/wivrn_session.cpp:564-581`). Mode/ceiling changes reset estimator and probe state (`bitrate_controller.cpp:365-389`; `flush_estimator():253-269`).
- Feedback is admitted only while automatic control, streaming, ceiling, positive period, and a video stream are valid (`bitrate_controller.cpp:781-803`). Frames join send bytes to per-stream feedback; BBR samples need at least 12 admitted loaded samples, with 20 window samples before evaluation; paced admission requires 30% of the configured window (`bitrate_controller.h:302-314, bitrate_controller.cpp:402-490, 846-860`). Radio hold blocks upward probes; loss and defined acute conditions cause backoff (`bitrate_controller.cpp:1126-1137,1214-1244`).
- Ordinary multi-stream BBR uses startup gain 1.25 until three flat 500 ms rounds, steady 0.85, then a 1.10 probe for 500 ms every 8 s. The probe is the bounded overshoot; post-probe samples are flushed and steady target is applied immediately, and acute backoff sets the next probe time to now (`bitrate_controller.h:316-355`, `.cpp:1257-1310,1330-1357`). On the modeled 500 Mbit/s trace, the steady target is 425 Mbit/s; the 550 Mbit/s probe has a nominal 500 ms timer with an 8 s retry interval before the scheduled drain; callback evaluation, sample admission and settling can delay that drain. This is the model's implied duty, not measured transport behavior.
- NX direct is a separate quality-budget specialization (`quality_mode` requires exactly one quality-tagged measured stream, `.cpp:1109-1112`). It uses the same per-stream utilization and rate samples; do not merge stereo timestamps. Its retry interval is 500 ms after each 500 ms probe, allowing alternating probe/steady periods (up to 50% probe time); clean probes raise `direct_probe_gain` by 0.02 up to 1.10. Acute events reset gain to 1.04, but the retry cadence remains 500 ms. The controller only grows this gain when loss/late are zero and the stream's utilization is below one frame period (`.cpp:1216-1222,1283-1308,1303-1309`). Thus direct mode can retry at the same gain repeatedly when it is not clean. Because quality mode holds its current target against lower estimated targets unless late feedback exists (`.cpp:1330-1335`), simply waiting longer would reduce probe frequency but would not itself lower an already over-period target.

## Decision and next gate

I do not recommend a generic cap or a change to ordinary BBR from this model: its probe is already time-bounded, radio/loss-aware, and followed by a steady drain. The distinct candidate is NX direct only: when a direct probe fails its clean-fit condition, delay the next quality probe by the existing 8 s `probe_interval`; let a subsequent clean probe resume the 500 ms, +0.02 quality ramp. The current 1.04 retry gain after acute loss could be reused as the “retry slowly” marker, but non-acute over-period results need an explicit marker or helper. Do not implement from the model alone; it does not prove direct ASTC payloads map to those spans.

Acceptance gate before considering a source change: use the actual controller/helper in a deterministic test with stable loaded capacity, constrained capacity, and a step-up recovery; show that repeated unclean direct probes stop, while a recovered link is rediscovered at the next bounded retry and quality gain resumes. Include loss, late/missing feedback, radio hold, ceiling/floor, and reset/mode-switch cases. Keep the ordinary two-stream and uneven/missing-eye BBR rate tests unchanged; verify utilization remains the maximum individual stream span, never the merged cross-eye span. Report missed samples explicitly: no feedback means the callback-driven controller cannot evaluate or discover capacity. The test would prove controller policy only, not queue clearance, real refresh deadlines, Wi-Fi throughput, or headset latency.

Root follow-up: the audit rejected a generic timer/gain cut. The separate native steady-state admission gate below uses existing late feedback to postpone upward probes; it leaves startup, already-active probes, loss backoff and NX-direct policy unchanged.
