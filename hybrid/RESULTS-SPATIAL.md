# The spatial hybrid: results

A hardware HEVC **periphery** plus an NX Warp **fovea inset**, composited
with a feathered boundary. Generated by `nxvc-hybridsim spatial`; the
design is in [../docs/SPATIAL-HYBRID.md](../docs/SPATIAL-HYBRID.md) and the
arrangement it is *not* is [ADR 0022](../docs/adr/0022-hybrid-mode-is-not-a-quality-tool.md).

Sequence: `vr-mixed-1024-v2.yuv420p.json`, 36 frames at 2048x1024 yuv420p side-by-side, 95.0 degree eye. 
Bitrates are quoted throughout as their **2 x 2048^2 x 90 Hz equivalent**.
The inset is coded by the real codec (`nxv-enc`/`nxv-dec`, intra + inter,
`build-ref/bin`); the periphery is x265 under the
zerolatency P-only settings of `nxvchybrid.base`, at full resolution
(ADR 0022 item 3).

FovVideoVDP: pico4: panel 2160x2160 per eye (17.27 ppd on axis), scoring 1024x1024 at 8.19 ppd, 95 deg FOV, 90 Hz, 100 nit sRGB, viewing distance 3 m (6.55 m wide); pyfvvdp 1.2.2 on cuda, foveated.

Feather: 32 px. Run time 20 min.

> numbers" at the end -- are written by hand on top of them, as in

## The answer, up front

1. **The spatial hybrid really does foveate, and hard.** Its best-fovea
   configuration opens a **+12.0 dB fovea-over-periphery gap** at 40 Mbit
   against x265's -2.6 dB, and lands a fovea PSNR of 50.82 dB against the
   foveated HEVC anchor's 51.35. The mechanism works: putting the real codec
   on a small centred crop buys a very high bit density exactly where the eye
   is sharpest.
2. **And it is beaten anyway, by an HEVC encoder with a delta-QP map.**
   `x265-p-refresh` reaches the same fovea quality (51.35 / 55.99 / 60.34 dB
   at 40 / 80 / 150 Mbit) *without* giving up the periphery -- its periphery
   is 47.69 dB where the spatial hybrid's is 38.86. It wins the
   eccentricity-weighted PSNR by **2.2 to 8.1 dB** and FovVideoVDP by
   **0.14 to 0.43 JOD** at every budget and every configuration tested.
3. **Against plain flat x265 it is a near-tie at best.** The best spatial
   point is 0.66 to 0.84 dB behind flat x265 on weighted PSNR at every
   budget. On JOD the 256-px inset at a 70 percent split edges it by
   **+0.013 JOD at 80 Mbit and +0.017 at 150** -- differences far inside
   anything a viewer could report, and negative at 40 Mbit.
4. **The optimum runs away from the interesting insets.** At every budget the
   best weighted score is the *smallest* inset tested (128 px in the
   simulator, 384 px on a Pico 4, +/-7.8 degrees) at the *largest* periphery
   share. That inset is smaller than the fovea disc the metric scores and far
   smaller than the eye box a headset without eye tracking needs. Every step
   toward an inset that would actually cover the eye box costs 1 to 5 dB.
5. **The reason is visible in the split columns.** A larger inset means more
   pixels at NX Warp's bit efficiency and fewer bits for the 90 percent of
   the picture x265 still has to code. The periphery column falls faster than
   the fovea column rises. This is ADR 0022's finding arriving by a different
   route: not "the optimiser retreats to HEVC", but "the region the codec is
   good for is smaller than the region the headset needs covered".
6. **The feather is cheap and slightly helpful.** 64 px beats 32 by 0.23 dB
   and a hard seam by 0.37 dB, at no cost anywhere. **Blanking the periphery
   under the inset is not worth it**: +8 QP inside the inset box is within
   noise (-0.01 dB) and +16 QP loses 0.56 dB, because the feather ring and
   the loss fallback both still need that region coded.
7. **The decoder cost model says the eye box does not fit 5 ms.** The
   1344 x 1088 px inset the Pico 4's fixed foveation asks for is 9.8 ms
   luma-only and 14.6 ms with chroma at the clock the bench measured. The
   largest 5 ms inset is 832 px. See the last two sections.

The reading, and what would change it, are at the end.

## Reading the numbers

### The comparison that decides it

Three arms, one budget, and the only honest way to line them up is by what
the eye gets for what the client has to run:

| arm | fovea | periphery | JOD | client decode | new code |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| x265 flat (WiVRn today) | 48.40 | 50.40 | 9.681 | 8-12 ms MediaCodec | none |
| **x265 + delta-QP map** | **55.99** | **51.67** | **9.937** | 8-12 ms MediaCodec | a quantization map |
| spatial hybrid, best JOD | 50.82 | 48.45 | 9.694 | 8-12 ms **plus** Pass A/B | everything in section 6 |
| spatial hybrid, best fovea | 55.81 | 44.82 | 9.535 | as above | as above |

(80 Mbit, from the tables above.)

The middle row is the problem. `VK_KHR_video_encode_quantization_map` shipped
in November 2024 and RADV implements it; `docs/RESEARCH-INDUSTRY.md` 2.2 is
blunt that a competitor can build a foveated, IDR-free hardware HEVC streamer
today. This experiment measures NX Warp's own foveation mechanism against
exactly that opponent, on the metric PAPER 5.3 names, and loses to it by
0.24 JOD while adding a second decoder to the client.

### Why the spatial hybrid loses despite foveating well

The delta-QP anchor foveates by *reallocating* bits inside one encode. The
spatial hybrid foveates by *removing* bits from one coder and giving them to
another. The first is free; the second pays the efficiency difference between
the two coders on every bit it moves, and pays it on the largest region of
the image. The fovea gain is real and roughly matches the anchor's; the
periphery loss is what the anchor does not have.

That difference is why the "does not transfer" argument of
[SPATIAL-HYBRID.md](../docs/SPATIAL-HYBRID.md) section 1 is only half right.
The layered optimum ran to plain HEVC because `base_frac` could remove the
weak coder entirely. The spatial optimum cannot do that -- and it still runs
to the smallest inset and the largest periphery share, which is the same
destination reached by a different road.

### What would change this answer

In rough order of how much they threaten the conclusion.

1. **The inset is coded flat.** No foveated sample scale, no `dQ_ecc`, no
   4:4:4 fovea -- the whole of PAPER 5.1.2's ladder is off inside the inset,
   which is the one place the codec claims an advantage the anchor cannot
   copy. This is the same "foveation is off" caveat ADR 0022 listed, and here
   it is more damaging, because the anchor it loses to *is* a foveation
   scheme. Turning it on would also cut the decoder cost of section 4 by
   roughly half. **This is the measurement to do next.**
2. **Loss is not modelled.** ADR 0022 called per-tile loss behaviour "the
   strongest remaining case" for hybrid mode and it is untouched here. The
   spatial split has a specific and testable claim: a lost inset degrades to
   the plain HEVC frame (a soft fovea for one frame), while a lost HEVC
   reference invalidates the whole picture. On a lossy link the ordering
   above could change. Nothing in this file says it does.
3. **`x265-p-refresh` emulates a quantization map, it is not one.** It is
   axis-aligned rectangles driven through `addroi` as an AQ *hint*, in CRF
   mode, which the rate controller may temper
   (`tools/quality/README.md`). A real per-block map would be at least as
   good, so the anchor is if anything understated -- the direction is safe.
4. **One sequence, 36 frames, one pose profile.** `vr-mixed-1024-v2`, mixed
   motion, band-limited. The content mix sets how much the codec's inter
   prediction earns.
5. **The simulator's eye is 1024 px over 95 degrees**, which is 8.2 ppd
   against the Pico 4's 22. Every inset here is translated to the headset by
   angle, but the *coding* problem at 8 ppd is not the coding problem at 22:
   the test clip's pixels carry higher spatial frequencies than the panel's
   would. This penalises both arms and it is not obvious which more.
6. **`nxv-enc` has no rate control.** The inset rate is hit by bisecting the
   frame QP, so the inset has no per-frame VBV and the periphery does. The
   x265 arms get a rate controller and the codec arm does not.
7. **Points marked `+` did not spend their budget.** At the smallest inset the
   codec saturates at QP 0 with bits left over; those bits went nowhere,
   where a real allocator would return them to the periphery. Every such
   point is therefore *pessimistic* -- and the winning configuration at
   40 and 80 Mbit is one of them, so the small-inset rows would look slightly
   better still, not worse. It does not reach the anchor.

### Recommendation

**A spatial hybrid does not make the Pico 4 a viable NX Warp target, and it
should not be built.** It works -- the inset genuinely concentrates quality on
the fovea, by up to 12 dB over its own periphery, and at 80 Mbit its fovea is
within 0.2 dB of the best foveated HEVC encode -- but it is beaten on the
paper's own primary metric by 0.14 to 0.43 JOD, at every budget and every
configuration, by an ordinary HEVC encoder with a delta-QP map, which needs no
second decoder on the client and no new code at all. The arrangement's own
optimum is an inset of about +/-8 degrees, which is smaller than the fovea and
far smaller than the 20 x 15 degree eye box a headset without eye tracking has
to cover; the inset that *does* cover the eye box costs 9.8 ms of Adreno 650
time (14.6 with chroma) against a 5 ms gate, and only survives because it
would hide under MediaCodec's own 8 to 12 ms -- which is a way of saying the
whole path inherits the latency ADR 0022 already priced. Building it would
touch the bitstream (`layer_desc` gains an inset rectangle), the encoder (a
second instance and a second rate target), `rc/` (two coupled targets plus the
periphery-IDR interaction), the transport (inset runs scheduled last, their
own FEC class), and on the client `nxwarp_hybrid.{h,cpp}` (~350 lines),
`reprojection.glsl` (one tap, a rect test, a feather lerp behind a
specialization constant), `stream_defoveator.cpp` (a second sampler binding),
and `android_decoder.{h,cpp}` (the low-latency keys, which are worth doing for
HEVC today regardless) -- perhaps two weeks of work for a result that is
0.24 JOD behind a `VkVideoEncodeQuantizationMapInfoKHR` struct. The one
finding worth keeping is the negative space around it: **the codec's fovea
coding is competitive and its periphery coding is what loses**, so the
measurement that should happen before this idea is closed for good is
foveation *inside* the inset (item 1 above), which is both the largest
unexploited lever in the sweep and the one thing the delta-QP anchor cannot
copy.
