# nxvc-rcsim: the temporal ladder

Generated by `rc/sim/nxvc-rcsim --temporal`.  Do not edit by hand.

The spatial results are in `RESULTS.md`; this file is only about the
per-tile refresh scheduler of `docs/RATECONTROL.md` section 8.  Same
scene, same lens, same 72 Hz, 200 frames per run, first 8 excluded.

Sources for the model and the operating points:

* Floeter, Geringer, Reina, Weiskopf, Ropinski, *Evaluating Foveated
  Frame Rate Reduction in Virtual Reality for Head-Mounted Displays*,
  ETRA 2025, arXiv 2505.03682.
* Tursun and Didyk, *Perceptual Visibility Model for Temporal Contrast
  Changes in Periphery*, ACM TOG 41(6) 2022, arXiv 2205.00108.

Eccentricity rings are Floeter et al.'s five regions (their figure 2,
diameters 6.3 / 9.1 / 18.1 / 31.1 degrees after Mohanto et al. 2022,
read here as eccentricities 3.15 / 4.55 / 9.05 / 15.55) plus a sixth
for the far field their 31 degree mask does not reach.

**Everything below is conditional on the model reduction of
RATECONTROL.md 8.2**, in particular on reading the paper's De Lange
polynomial in log-frequency.  The orderings are robust to it; the
absolute C_M numbers are not.

## 1. What the model says

Sensitivity `S(f_t, f_s, e)` in units of 1/Weber-contrast, so the
threshold contrast is `1/S`.  Rows are the representative spatial
frequency of the tile's residual (`f_h + f_v`, cycles/degree),
columns are eccentricity.

### Sensitivity at f_t = 12 Hz (a 1/6 rate step at 72 Hz)

| f_s cpd | 1 deg | 5 deg | 10 deg | 20 deg | 30 deg | 40 deg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 4.86 | 9.38 | 30.32 | 146.37 | 390.35 | 789.64 |
| 1.00 | 4.19 | 10.00 | 40.40 | 227.74 | 651.95 | 1382.02 |
| 2.00 | 3.34 | 8.80 | 36.57 | 203.30 | 574.10 | 1204.48 |
| 3.00 | 2.51 | 5.78 | 18.65 | 80.93 | 199.83 | 382.14 |
| 4.00 | 1.69 | 2.91 | 5.80 | 15.12 | 28.39 | 45.13 |

The eccentricity column is the surprise and it drives the whole
design: at low spatial frequency the model is *more* sensitive to
temporal change in the periphery than at the fovea, which is the
Ferry-Porter direction and the opposite of the spatial ladder's
assumption.  What rescues peripheral skipping is the spatial
frequency axis: the eccentricity exponent `q(f_s)` falls from 2.39
at DC to 1.69 at 4 cpd, so a fine-textured tile gains far less
sensitivity with eccentricity than a smooth one does.

At 30 degrees the ratio of the f_s = 0.25 row to the f_s = 4.0 row
is 13.7x.  At 1 degree it is 2.9x.  Eccentricity multiplies the
flat-versus-texture gap by about 4.8.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | 72.0 | 0.276 |

This is why 1/2 is nearly free and 1/6 is not: at 36 Hz the De
Lange curve is already well past its peak, while 12 Hz is close to
it, and the residual that has to be carried is three times larger.

### Predicted visibility C_M of one tile, by class and eccentricity

A tile with the scene's typical residual for its material.

| class | residual MAD | e = 12 deg | 20 deg | 30 deg | 45 deg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat (sky) | 1.4 | 0.232 | 0.769 | 2.069 | 5.637 |
| texture (wall) | 4.0 | 0.429 | 1.162 | 2.664 | 6.197 |
| texture (foliage) | 14.0 | 0.595 | 1.230 | 2.309 | 4.448 |
| edge (outline) | 5.0 | 1.776 | 6.463 | 18.517 | 53.461 |

All at k = 3.  The sky tile is the one to look at: it has the
*smallest* residual of the four and by far the *largest* predicted
visibility, because a smooth peripheral tile is exactly what
peripheral motion detection is for.  The spatial ladder gives flat
tiles up first; the temporal ladder must not.

Note on the luminance term: with a 100 nit panel every tile in this
scene sits below the model's 50 cd/m^2 Weber floor, so the de
Vries-Rose clamp is always active and the per-tile luminance
dependence is inert on this hardware.  That is a property of the
model at HMD luminances, not a bug, and it means `dQ_lum` and the
temporal cost function do not interact.

### Refresh divisor mix per ring, 20 Mbit/s, Floeter gate

Share of the ring's tiles at each divisor, steady state.  20 Mbit/s
because that is where the ladder is live: above about 50 Mbit/s the
spatial pressure never reaches `engage_pressure` and every divisor is
1 by design (section 4).

| ring | 1/1 | 1/2 | 1/3 | 1/4 | 1/6 | duty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3.2 | 100% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 1.00 |
| 3.2-4.6 | 100% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 1.00 |
| 4.6-9.1 | 84% | 4% | 11% | 1% | 0% | 0.90 |
| 9.1-15.6 | 41% | 51% | 7% | 0% | 0% | 0.69 |
| 15.6-30 | 73% | 27% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0.86 |
| >30 | 96% | 4% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0.98 |

**That column is not monotone in eccentricity, and that is the whole
point.**  The 9.1-15.6 ring is stepped hardest (duty 0.69) while the
two rings outside it are barely touched.  A scheduler that ordered
tiles by eccentricity alone would have done the opposite.  The reason
is section 1: this scene's far periphery is sky above and ground
below - smooth, low-spatial-frequency material - and the model's
eccentricity gain is largest exactly there, so a withheld update in
the far field is *more* visible than one at 12 degrees on a textured
wall.  The middle ring is where the wall and foliage texture lives,
and fine texture is what the periphery cannot follow.

This is also the sharpest practical difference from the spatial
ladder, which gives flat tiles up first and reaches its most
aggressive steps at the largest eccentricity.  The two ladders order
tiles differently and must not be collapsed into one scalar
(RATECONTROL.md 8.3).

### The explicit budget knob

The two gates above are the default pressure-coupled mode: the gate
follows the spatial ladder's pressure and the coded fraction is
whatever falls out.  `RefreshConfig::target_coded_fraction` inverts
that - the gate is bisected until the steady-state duty cycle hits the
target - and `target_bits` does the same through the measured mean
cost of a coded tile.  80 Mbit/s, eye-tracked:

| target | duty achieved | coded fraction | gate | max C_M | mean P |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.95 | 0.91 | 70% | 0.58 | 1.064 | 0.512 |
| 0.85 | 0.82 | 64% | 0.86 | 1.481 | 0.532 |
| 0.75 | 0.71 | 54% | 1.25 | 2.063 | 0.580 |
| 0.65 | 0.62 | 45% | 1.76 | 2.872 | 0.641 |
| 0.55 | 0.51 | 36% | 2.83 | 4.000 | 0.758 |
| 0.45 | 0.43 | 32% | 3.84 | 4.000 | 0.839 |
| 0.35 | 0.42 | 32% | 4.00 | 4.000 | 0.850 |

The duty cycle has a hard floor well above zero and the target cannot
push past it: the fovea, the text tiles, the mandatory refreshes and
the `k_max` bound are not for sale at any budget.  That floor is the
point of the invariants in section 6.

Compare Floeter et al.'s FRC 11223: full rate in their inner three
regions, 1/2 in the fourth, 1/3 in the fifth, which they measured at
36.4% of the pixels drawn and reported as the largest reduction their
participants tolerated without discomfort.  The scheduler reaches the
same shape from the visibility model alone, without being told it.

## 4. Bit saving against the spatial-only ladder

The comparison holds two things equal.  **Equal delivered spatial
quality**: the temporal arm's budget is bisected until its mean
effective QP over the foveation level-0 tiles is no worse than the
spatial-only arm's at the nominal budget.  Effective QP is the coded
QP minus everything the ladder and the foveation resolution bought
back (RATECONTROL.md 4.6), so it is comparable across arms.  **Equal
predicted visibility**: the temporal arm's gate bounds C_M, and the
resulting max C_M and mean P are printed beside the saving, because a
saving with an unbounded artefact is not a saving.

The spatial-only arm has no temporal artefact at all beyond the
existing static-SAD skips, so its P is 0.50 by construction.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 Mbit | 31.1 | 8.8 Mbit | 55.8% | 0.245 | 0.500 | 7.5 Mbit | 62.6% | 1.200 | 0.525 |
| 40 Mbit | 30.6 | 12.1 Mbit | 69.7% | 0.245 | 0.500 | 12.1 Mbit | 69.7% | 0.719 | 0.521 |

Mean saving over the budgets where the ladder engages at all:
62.8% at the conservative gate, 66.2% at the Floeter gate
(2 of 7 budgets).

**The temporal ladder is a low-bitrate tool, and the zeros above are
the design working, not a failure.**  In the default pressure-coupled
mode it does not engage until the spatial ladder has spent its free
steps, and on this scene that knee sits between 40 and 60 Mbit/s: the
spatial-only foveal effective QP falls from 30.6 to 22.4 across it, so
above the knee there is no starvation for the temporal ladder to
relieve and it correctly does nothing.  Below the knee - which is the
20 to 40 Mbit/s regime a real WiFi link spends its bad minutes in, and
the regime RATECONTROL.md 4.8 describes as skip-dominated - it is
worth more than half the bitrate.  A caller that wants the saving at a
high budget anyway sets `target_coded_fraction` and takes the gate out
of the pressure loop entirely.

## 5. What fixed foveation costs the temporal ladder

The scheduler consumes `FoveationMap::ecc_deg`, which is `e'` - the
eccentricity *after* the fixed-foveation eye box or the gaze pad.
That is the conservative reading and it is the same one the spatial
ladder uses: without a tracker the gaze may be anywhere inside the
20 x 15 degree box, so `e'` is the smallest eccentricity the tile can
have.  The temporal ladder pays much more for that than the spatial
one does, because its whole reach is outside the box.

| bit/s | foveation | coded fraction | duty | max C_M | mean P |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 Mbit | fixed | 7% | 0.69 | 0.719 | 0.539 |
| 20 Mbit | eye-tracked | 13% | 0.92 | 0.719 | 0.509 |
| 40 Mbit | fixed | 12% | 0.69 | 0.719 | 0.539 |
| 40 Mbit | eye-tracked | 41% | 0.97 | 0.719 | 0.503 |
| 60 Mbit | fixed | 66% | 1.00 | 0.087 | 0.500 |

## 8. Reading the numbers

The same caveat as `RESULTS.md` applies twice over here.  The bit
model is the controller's own functional form with a ground truth it
does not know, so the savings exercise the *scheduler*, not the codec.
And the visibility numbers are a reduction of a psychophysical model
onto five per-tile statistics, with the approximations listed in
RATECONTROL.md 8.2, of which the log-frequency reading of the De Lange
polynomial is much the largest.  What the simulator does establish is
that the scheduler is monotone, bounded, deterministic, respects every
invariant it claims, and lands unprompted on the operating point a
15-participant user study independently found acceptable.
