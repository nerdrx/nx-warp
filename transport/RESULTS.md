### Why the numbers differ

**Datagrams per frame.** TRANSPORT.md decision D3 makes a run homogeneous in
layer, `ref_delta`, tile class, band and tile row, because `ref_delta` and
`layer_id` live in the datagram header while references are chosen per tile.
A tile row is 68 tiles and crosses two or three foveation classes, so runs end
at class boundaries long before the 1316-byte payload budget is reached: about
9 tiles per run instead of the paper's 20.  That doubles both the datagram rate
and the per-tile header cost.  Removing the class from the run key (moving it
into the tile directory and grouping FEC per datagram rather than per class)
would recover most of it and is the obvious v1.1 change.

**Overhead.** The paper's 5.5 %% is `(24 + 20*4) / (104 + 1800)`.  It omits the
16-byte AEAD tag its own section 4.1 mandates, the 26-byte pose header its
section 6.7 replicates per band, and IPv4/UDP.  With 9-tile runs, a tag and a
pose header the honest figure is 8.7 %% before FEC and 26.7 %% on the wire.

**FEC.** 14.5 %% assumes every group is a full k = 10.  Groups may not cross a
band or a class (4.4, decision D6), and a band holds only about 29 datagrams
split three ways, so class A groups run at k = 3 to 5 with m = 3: a 60 to 100 %%
parity ratio, not 30 %%.  Parity blocks are also padded to the longest datagram
in the group.  Measured blended parity is 21 %%.  Either the parity counts have
to scale with k, or FEC groups have to span the whole frame's class A traffic
and accept the extra latency.

**Band latency and reference distance.** The paper's 4.2 timeline gives the air
3 ms and no serialisation time at all: 208 KB at 300 Mbit/s takes 5.5 ms to
clock out, which is most of a 11.1 ms frame.  At 300 Mbit/s of usable air rate
the queue never empties inside a frame, the deadline controller saturates at its
4 ms limit, and feedback for frame N arrives too late for frame N+1, so 97.6 %%
of tiles reference N-2 rather than the bottom bands only.  Give the link 600
Mbit/s and band latency drops to 7.5 ms and 99.6 %% of tiles reference N-1.  The
paper's latency and reference-distance claims are claims about a link with about
3x headroom over the stream, and should say so.

**Deadline direction.** PAPER 4.3 says the deadline "moves 1 ms earlier ...
trading latency for fewer holes".  Moving it earlier gives less time for
datagrams and therefore more holes.  Decision D16 implements it as moving later,
which is what the stated trade means; with the paper's sign the simulator
conceals every tile of every frame on the 300 Mbit/s link.

**Late tiles.** PAPER 4.3 item 5 makes a late tile a valid reference.  Decision
D17 decodes it for display but does not acknowledge it, because otherwise the
client holds pixels the sender's shadow believes are concealed and prediction
diverges silently - exactly what 4.5 exists to prevent.  With D17 the shadow is
bit-exact in every scenario and every fuzz seed; without it the simulator shows
thousands of divergent tiles per run.

**Repaired bytes.** A FEC group is repaired as soon as k blocks are present, so
a group whose members are merely reordered gets repaired before the stragglers
arrive.  The parenthesised figure is the part of the repair that was actually
needed (the original never arrived); the rest is wasted GF(256) work and is the
argument for deferring recovery until a parity datagram has arrived.

**USB.** The 20 to 50 ms RNDIS/NCM stalls of PAPER 4.11 fill a 512 KB socket
buffer in about 21 ms at this rate, so a single-path USB user loses a burst to
tail drop rather than riding the stall out.  Multipath answers it: with WiFi as
the second path, late tiles fall from 5.6 to 3.8 per frame and band latency p50
is 4.9 ms.
