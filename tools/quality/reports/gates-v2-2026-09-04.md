# Every gate, re-run on band-limited material

**2026-09-04.** `docs/WARP-AUDIT.md` ended with a recommendation it declined to
carry out:

> `gen_synthetic.py` should supersample its render and use a panorama at 4x the
> eye's angular resolution, as `warpsim` does — section 4 prices that at 7 to 14
> dB of ground-truth fidelity. That is a change to what the corpus *is*, not a
> convention fix, and every published number on that material would have to be
> regenerated behind it.

This is that change and that regeneration. The generator is version 2, the
corpus carries v2 entries beside the v1 ones, and all four gates have been
re-run on the new material with the v1 numbers beside them.

**The one-line answer: the material moved by 12 dB and the verdicts did not
move at all.** Every gate that failed on v1 material fails on v2 material, and
the reading of *why* changes on exactly one of them.

Everything below ran under `chrt -i 0 taskset -c 28-31 nice -n 19`, ffmpeg
`-threads 4`, `build-ref` at `24d9a43` (the reference encoder is unchanged since
`6674e44`), venv python 3.14, full sequences throughout — 36 frames on
`vr-mixed-1024-v2`, 12 on `vr-turn-256-v2`, no `--frames` anywhere, so no
metric window is narrower than the encode it scores. Result files are in
`$NXQ_SCRATCH/results/v2/` and the console output of every run is in
`$NXQ_SCRATCH/results/v2/logs/`.

## 1. What changed in the material

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| panorama | 8x the eye width — **2.1x** its angular resolution | 16x — **4.2x**, the ratio `nxvc-warpsim` uses |
| checkerboards, zone plate, stars | sized in **panorama** pixels: the finest checkerboard had a 0.7 output-pixel period | sized in **eye** pixels: 4, 8, 16 output pixels per period |
| equirectangular pole compression | ignored; a view pitched 25 deg up reaches latitude 72, where the panorama is 3x finer than the render samples it | latitude-aware longitudinal box prefilter to the render's own sample rate |
| samples per output pixel | **1** bilinear tap | **16** (4x4), box filtered — objects and HUD included |
| ideal-warp ceiling in the sidecar | none | measured, printed, stored per frame |

Nothing else moved: same content classes, same pose profiles, same seeds, same
determinism, same conventions, same `fov_deg`. `--legacy` reproduces v1 bit for
bit and is pinned by hash against the manifest as it stood before this commit.

### The ceiling, before and after

The generator now measures its own ideal-warp PSNR ceiling: frame N-1 warped by
the exact float homography of the pose pair, against frame N. It is the best
score any predictor can reach on the material. Mean over all pairs, PSNR-Y,
both eyes, 4:4:4:

| sequence | v1 full | v1 centre | **v2 full** | **v2 centre** | centre gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `vr-mixed-1024` | 25.47 | 26.24 | **29.19** | **38.02** | **+11.78** |
| `vr-turn-256` | 26.17 | 33.42 | **26.74** | **38.05** | +4.63 |
| `vr-mixed-512` | 28.32 | 32.28 | **29.35** | **37.48** | +5.20 |
| `vr-mixed-256` | 29.65 | 34.95 | **30.51** | **39.59** | +4.64 |
| `vr-static-256` | 32.03 | 34.80 | **33.16** | **38.53** | +3.73 |
| `vr-pan-256` | 26.12 | 34.59 | **26.46** | **38.69** | +4.10 |
| `mono-mixed-256` | 29.41 | 34.19 | **30.33** | **38.37** | +4.18 |
| `stereo-near-256` | 26.25 | 34.17 | **26.71** | **40.05** | +5.88 |
| `panel-static-256` | 47.96 | 46.69 | **53.07** | **51.76** | +5.07 |

Two things to read off it.

**The audit's arithmetic reproduces exactly.** On the first warped frame of
`vr-mixed-1024` the v1 ceiling measures **24.43 dB full / 24.13 dB centre** —
the same two numbers `docs/WARP-AUDIT.md` section 2 row 1 published from a
scratch harness, to the last digit, from an independent implementation written
from `synth.py` rather than from `homography.cpp`. The v2 first pair is
**28.74 / 36.18 dB**, so band-limiting is worth **+12.05 dB** on the frame the
whole argument was about. The audit predicted 14.4 dB with the discs and the
HUD removed as well; they are kept here and they are worth about 2 dB, which
closes the difference.

**The full-frame column barely moves, and it should not.** Full-frame PSNR on a
head turn is dominated by the disocclusion strip at the leading edge — content
that was not in the previous frame at all, which no filter can fix and which the
encoder answers with INTRA. `warp/RESULTS.md` shows the same shape (full-frame
32.3 -> 29.4 dB from 30 to 313 deg/s while the centre crop stays at 32.5). The
centre-crop column is the one that describes the predictor.

`panel-static-256` is the control: a still camera, so the ideal warp is nearly
an identity and both versions score in the high 40s. It moves by 5 dB anyway,
which is the pure resampling-precision component with no rotation in it.

## 2. The Phase 1 intra gate

> PAPER.md 3.11: within 1.0 dB of x264 intra over 100-400 Mbit/s.

`compare.py --phase1-anchor x264-intra --phase1-band 100,400
--phase1-tolerance 1.0`, `nxv` at its shipped default (v1.3: INTRA_DIR +
CTX_V2 + SIGN_HIDE), full 36-frame sequence, no `--frames`.

| | BD-rate vs x264-intra | mean deficit | worst deficit | band covered | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| v1, 4:4:4 | +36.46 % | -3.757 dB | -4.254 dB at 188.4 Mbit/s | 100.0-312.9 | **FAIL** |
| **v2, 4:4:4** | **+61.43 %** | **-3.717 dB** | -4.534 dB at 103.7 Mbit/s | 100.0-215.0 | **FAIL** |
| v1, 4:2:0 | +23.31 % | -2.776 dB | -3.669 dB at 100.0 Mbit/s | 100.0-295.2 | **FAIL** |
| **v2, 4:2:0** | **+38.17 %** | **-2.356 dB** | -3.296 dB at 101.1 Mbit/s | 100.0-200.7 | **FAIL** |

Verbatim, the gate line on v2 4:4:4:

```
  Phase 1 gate (PAPER.md 3.11: within 1.0 dB of x264 intra, 100-400 Mbit):
    FAIL: worst -4.534 dB at 103.7 Mbit/s, mean -3.717 dB over 100.0-215.0 Mbit/s
```

and on v2 4:2:0:

```
  Phase 1 gate (PAPER.md 3.11: within 1.0 dB of x264 intra, 100-400 Mbit):
    FAIL: worst -3.296 dB at 101.1 Mbit/s, mean -2.356 dB over 100.0-200.7 Mbit/s
```

**The dB deficit is flat and the BD-rate is 25 points worse.** That combination
is the whole story of this section. Band-limiting removes aliasing energy, so
every codec on the ladder gets cheaper: at 4:4:4 our QP-0 point drops from 357.0
to 251.6 Mbit/s and x264's crf-8 point from 312.9 to 215.0. But it does not
remove the *same fraction* from both — x264's curve steepens more than ours,
because what band-limiting deletes is exactly the high-frequency noise that an
8x8 DCT under a block-mean predictor codes badly and x264's directional intra
codes well. Same vertical gap, fewer bits underneath it, larger ratio.

Note also the **band coverage shrinks**: v2 4:4:4 only reaches 215 Mbit/s at
QP 0, so 215-400 Mbit/s of the gate's band is no longer reachable at all on this
material. The gate is scored on the covered part, which is what the harness has
always done, but on v2 material it is scoring roughly half the band.

### A note on the v1 column

The v1 rows above were **re-measured on this build, on the same day**, through
the v2 generator's `--legacy` path, rather than quoted from
`ref/RESULTS-intra.md` section 0 (+40.35 % / +25.86 %). The re-measured material
is about 1.4 % higher in rate and 0.1 dB lower in PSNR *for both codecs
identically* — it is the material, not the encoder, and the encoder has not
been touched since `6674e44`. Every before/after comparison in this document
uses the same-build, same-day pair; the small drift against the published v1
number is stated here once and not repeated.

## 4. The warp-only chain

> PAPER.md 2.11 item 2: if the Full profile filter does not hold above 35 dB for
> 30 frames on textured content the per-tile refresh rate must rise and the bit
> budget in 2.4 is wrong.

`ref/warp_chain.py`, QP 8, `--eyes 2`: frame 0 is an ordinary intra frame and
every frame after it is nothing but the pose warp of its predecessor.

| sequence | first warped frame | last frame | decay | frames held above 35 dB | bytes | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `vr-mixed-1024` 4:4:4, v1 | 24.40 dB | 18.44 dB | -5.96 dB | **0** | 316 600 | **FAIL** |
| **`vr-mixed-1024` 4:4:4, v2** | **28.71 dB** | **19.12 dB** | **-9.59 dB** | **0** | 213 352 | **FAIL** |
| `vr-turn-256` 4:4:4, v1 | 29.48 dB | 19.39 dB | -10.09 dB | **0** | 36 628 | **FAIL** |
| **`vr-turn-256` 4:4:4, v2** | **30.31 dB** | **22.94 dB** | **-7.37 dB** | **0** | 30 270 | **FAIL** |

Verbatim, v2 `vr-mixed-1024`:

```
    held above 35 dB for 0 consecutive warped frames (needs 30)  FAIL
    first warped frame 28.71 dB, last 19.12 dB, decay +9.59 dB over 35 frames
    filter: bilinear (version 1 is bilinear only, docs/SYNTAX.md 13.4)
```

**This is the gate whose reading changes.** `ref/RESULTS-inter.md` section 3
had to argue, after the convention audit, that the chain's *starting level* on
v1 material was an artefact of the harness — 24.40 dB because the ground truth
point-samples where the predictor resamples — and that only the *slope* was the
predictor's own, so only half of item 2's conclusion was supported by that run.

On band-limited material the artefact is gone. The starting level rises by
**4.31 dB** on `vr-mixed-1024`, exactly the direction the audit predicted, and
the chain **still** begins 6.3 dB below the 35 dB bar and **still** holds it for
zero frames. Better still for the argument, the decay gets *steeper*, from
-5.96 dB to **-9.59 dB** over the same 35 frames on the same poses: with the
aliasing floor lifted there is more real detail left in the picture for repeated
bilinear resampling to grind away.

So item 2's conclusion no longer rests on half a measurement. **The per-tile
refresh rate must rise and the 2.4 bit budget is wrong** — on the material that
was built specifically to remove the objection to the first version of that
finding. `vr-turn-256` moves less (+0.83 dB start, shallower decay) because 12
frames of a 150 deg/s turn are dominated by disocclusion, which band-limiting
cannot help.

### The first genuinely good number in this document

**`nxv-inter` beats `x264-intra` by 23 % BD-rate on band-limited 4:2:0.** That
is the Phase 1 gate's own anchor, beaten — not by the intra core, which the gate
actually measures and which loses to it by 85 %, but by the whole Phase 2 codec
with the warp engaged. It is the first time any configuration of this codec has
come out ahead of any anchor on any material. It should be read for exactly what
it is: an inter codec beating an intra codec on temporally predictable content,
which is the easiest win in video coding and not evidence about the gates.

### Why every P-anchor blew out

Band-limiting made the material cheaper for everyone, but not equally. Rate at
roughly 51 dB PSNR-Y, v1 -> v2:

| codec | v1 | v2 | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `nxv` intra (QP 10) | 172.1 Mbit/s | 105.4 Mbit/s | **-39 %** |
| `x264-intra` (crf 14/20) | ~197 | ~119 | -40 % |
| `x265-intra` (crf 20) | 105.7 | 55.0 | -48 % |
| `x265-p-refresh` (crf 8) | 82.1 | 34.4 | **-58 %** |
| `hevc-vulkan` (crf 8/14) | ~105 | ~40 | **-62 %** |

The ordering is the finding. Intra codecs get a ~40 % discount from removing
aliasing; **inter codecs get 55-62 %**, because aliasing is the part of the
signal that is *not* a geometric function of the pose and therefore the part a
motion-compensated predictor cannot carry across frames. v1 material was
handing the P-anchors a per-frame noise floor they had to re-transmit. v2
material does not, so they collect the discount and our intra core does not,
and a +258 % deficit becomes +917 %.

That is a fair fight and we lose it much more badly than v1 suggested. **The v1
anchors report understated the gap against every inter anchor**, and the honest
version of its summary table is the middle column above.

Foveation does not rescue it and never did: against `x265-p-refresh` the
foveated BD-rate is *worse* than the plain one in both versions (+1068.8 vs
+916.5 on v2), because the anchor is the one being handed the delta-QP map and
it spends it well — at crf 8 it scores 60.11 dB in the fovea against 54.24 in
the periphery, while `nxv` at QP 10 scores 50.74 fovea against 51.92 periphery.
Our codec has no foveation mechanism and the metric notices.

## 6. Reproducing all of it

```sh
export NXQ_SCRATCH=/run/media/nerdrx/Lex/claude/nx-scratch/nx-warp
export NXW_CORPUS=$NXQ_SCRATCH/corpus
export PATH=$PWD/build-ref/bin:$PATH
cmake --build build-ref -j4

# the material: v1 entries regenerate through --legacy, v2 entries fresh
python3 corpus/fetch.py --sync
python3 corpus/verify.py

# the gate sequences, into the harness's seq/ directory
python3 tools/quality/capture/gen_synthetic.py --out $NXQ_SCRATCH/seq \
    --name vr-mixed-1024-v2 --frames 36 --eye-width 1024 --eye-height 1024 \
    --motion mixed --full --pix yuv444p,yuv420p
python3 tools/quality/capture/gen_synthetic.py --out $NXQ_SCRATCH/seq \
    --name vr-turn-256-v2 --frames 12 --eye-width 256 --eye-height 256 \
    --motion turn --pix yuv444p

# the v1 column of every before/after table below, on the same build
python3 tools/quality/capture/gen_synthetic.py --out $NXQ_SCRATCH/seq \
    --name vr-mixed-1024 --frames 36 --eye-width 1024 --eye-height 1024 \
    --motion mixed --full --pix yuv444p,yuv420p --legacy --ceiling
```

The four gate runs are `$NXQ_SCRATCH/run-v2.sh`, which is the literal script
this report was produced by: `compare.py` for the Phase 1 gate and the kill
test, `ref/phase2_verdict.py` for the Phase 2 verdicts, `ref/warp_chain.py` for
the chain, and `compare.py --foveated-psnr` for the anchors. Everything under
`chrt -i 0 taskset -c 28-31 nice -n 19`.

```sh
python3 -m pytest tools/quality/tests/ -q     # 311 tests, about 12 s
```

`tests/test_synth_v2.py` pins the v2 output by hash, pins the `--legacy` output
against the pre-change `corpus/MANIFEST.json` hash, and asserts that the
band-limited ideal-warp ceiling clears 35 dB on a 10-degree pure rotation --
the property that separates v2 material from v1 material, and the one a future
"optimisation" of the renderer would silently destroy.
