# NX Warp against the hardware-class anchors

Generated 2026-09-04 by `tools/quality/compare.py`. Sources:
`nx-scratch/nx-warp/results/anchors-*.json`, one file per row of the table below.

`docs/RESEARCH-INDUSTRY.md` 2.2 sets the terms of this report. Vulkan has
ratified per-block quantization maps (`VK_KHR_video_encode_quantization_map`,
Nov 2024) and intra refresh (`VK_KHR_video_encode_intra_refresh`, Jul 2025),
and RADV implements both. A foveated, IDR-free hardware HEVC streamer is
therefore buildable today, by somebody else, without a new codec. Measuring
NX Warp against naive flat-QP HEVC would be measuring against a strawman, so
this run adds anchors that are as close to that opponent as this machine can
get.

## What was available on this machine

Radeon RX 7900 XTX, Mesa RADV 26.2.1, ffmpeg n9.0.1, all encodes under
`chrt -i 0 taskset -c 8-11 nice -n 19` with ffmpeg `-threads 4`.

Three findings sit in that table rather than in the numbers.

**The driver has the extensions; ffmpeg does not expose them.** `vulkaninfo`
reports `VK_KHR_video_encode_intra_refresh` (rev 1) and
`VK_KHR_video_encode_quantization_map` (rev 2) on RADV, alongside
`VK_KHR_video_encode_h264`/`_h265`/`_av1` and the
`IMAGE_LAYOUT_VIDEO_ENCODE_QUANTIZATION_MAP_KHR` layout. But
`ffmpeg -h encoder=hevc_vulkan` lists only `idr_interval`, `b_depth`,
`async_depth`, `qp`, `quality`, `rc_mode`, `tune`, `usage`, `content`,
`profile`, `tier`, `level` and `units`. There is no quantization-map option and
no intra-refresh option. The real extensions cannot be driven from this
toolchain, so `hevc-vulkan` and `h264-vulkan` here are **flat-QP, IDR-free-only**
Vulkan encodes, and the foveation and intra-refresh opponent is emulated on
x264/x265 instead.

**The emulation only works on CRF, and that is not a detail.** The
`*-p-refresh` anchors install a concentric delta-QP map (centre QP−6, middle QP,
periphery QP+6, one fovea per eye) through ffmpeg's `addroi` filter, which the
libx264 and libx265 wrappers turn into a per-macroblock/per-CTU quantizer
offset array — the same granularity of control the Vulkan extension provides.
Under **constant QP** both encoders switch adaptive quantization off, the
offsets are silently discarded, and the encode comes out **bit-identical** to
the flat one (verified: 39717 bytes with and without the map). The harness
therefore forces these anchors onto CRF, uses the run's numbers as CRF values,
and records `rate_control_note` in the results JSON. The `fovea − periphery`
column in the tables below is the check that it actually bit: the flat anchors
sit within a couple of tenths of a dB, the refresh anchors open **4 to 10 dB**
(7 to 10 on the two full-size sequences).

**No hardware path can carry 4:4:4 at all.** `hevc_vulkan` and `h264_vulkan`
take `nv12`/`p010` only and refuse to open on a 4:4:4 source; `libsvtav1` is
4:2:0-only in practice. On the two 4:4:4 sequences all three skip. That is not
a harness limitation to work around — it is the capability line
`docs/RESEARCH-INDUSTRY.md` 1.3 calls the entry ticket for
passthrough-composited streaming, showing up as a hard failure in a measurement
run.

## Codec under test

`build-ref/bin/nxv`, **intra-only**. Phase 2 inter prediction is landing
concurrently but `nxv-enc` has no `--inter` option in this build (`unknown
option --inter`), so there is no second codec row. Every comparison against a
P-frame anchor below is intra-against-inter and should be read as a floor, not
as a verdict on the design.

Operating points: `nxv` at QP 10–30, anchors at QP/CRF 8–44, chosen so the
quality ranges overlap and BD-rate is computable rather than extrapolated.

## The foveated metric

`--foveated-psnr` re-reads every curve through `foveated_metrics.py` with a
**centre fixation** — where the anchors put their low-QP box — at
`ppd_center` derived from a 95-degree view (8.19 ppd for the 1024-wide eye,
4.10 for the 512-wide, 2.05 for the 256-wide). Three columns come out of it:
`fov-PSNR-Y` (acuity-weighted, `1/(1 + e/2.3)`), and plain PSNR inside and
outside the 8-degree fovea disc. For `sbs` sequences each eye is weighted about
its own centre and combined in the MSE domain.

## The reading

Measured against the opponent the industry can actually build today, NX Warp is
not close yet, and the gap is worst exactly where the project claims its
differentiator. Like for like — intra against intra — `nxv` needs 40 to 66
percent more bits than x264 intra and 61 to 130 percent more than x265 intra,
which is a real improvement on the previous baseline (the Phase 1 deficit on
`vr-mixed-1024` 4:4:4 has come in from −10.31 dB to −4.15 dB) but still a clear
FAIL of the 1.0 dB gate. Against the P-frame anchors the 160 to 450 percent
figures are not yet meaningful, because this build has no inter prediction at
all; they are the size of the hole Phase 2 has to fill, not a verdict. The
number that should sting is the fovea column: a stock x265 driven with periodic
intra refresh and a three-band delta-QP map — no new codec, no new hardware,
about fifteen lines of ffmpeg — opens a 7 to 10 dB gap between fovea and
periphery on the full-size sequences and beats `nxv` by 470 to 950 percent on
foveal PSNR, while paying only about 22 to 34 percent in flat PSNR-Y rate for
the privilege (the ratio of the two BD-rate columns above). Foveation
and IDR-free recovery are, on this evidence, no longer differentiators; they are
table stakes that a competitor gets from `VK_KHR_video_encode_quantization_map`
and `VK_KHR_video_encode_intra_refresh` for free. What the run does confirm is
where the moat actually is, and it is not in the RD tables: all three hardware
and hardware-class anchors — `hevc_vulkan`, `h264_vulkan`, `libsvtav1` — refuse
to open on a 4:4:4 source at all, and ffmpeg exposes no way to reach the two
Vulkan extensions even though RADV implements them. The honest positioning
after this run is the one 2.2 already argues for: stop leading with foveation
and loss recovery, lead with 4:4:4, alpha, per-tile references and partial
frame presentation, which no pixel-rate or extension improvement on the encode
side provides — and get inter prediction landed before quoting any of these
numbers to anyone.

## Caveats

* Six-to-twelve-frame synthetic clips. Intra refresh has room for exactly one
  sweep, which flatters neither side, and a P-frame anchor's first-frame IDR is
  a sixth of its bits on `vr-mixed-1024`. Real captures will move all of these.
* The `*-p-refresh` anchors run on CRF while the flat anchors run on constant
  QP. The RD curves are still directly comparable — BD-rate integrates rate
  against quality and does not care how the point was parameterised — but the
  operating points are not matched pairs and should not be read as such.
* `fov-PSNR-Y` with the default `acuity` weighting barely separates the foveated
  anchors from the flat ones (0.5 to 0.7 dB), because `1/(1 + e/2.3)` still
  gives the periphery most of the weight by area. The hard fovea/periphery split
  is the discriminating view at these resolutions; `--fov-weighting acuity2` is
  the one to try next.
* The test material is downscaled, so `ppd_center` is 2 to 8 rather than a
  headset's ~16, and the 8-degree fovea disc is a smaller fraction of the frame
  than it would be in a real render target.
* `av1-svt-p` at preset 10 saturates: its curve flattens above ~45 dB and no CRF
  buys more quality. Read it as "what Virtual Desktop ships at a streaming
  preset", not as AV1's ceiling.
* `hevc-vulkan` also saturates, topping out near 54 dB where x265-p reaches 57.
  Hardware encode has a quality ceiling software does not.
